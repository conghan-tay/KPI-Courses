From the knowledge base below, write 12 multiple-choice questions, 4 options each.

WHAT THIS QUIZ IS FOR
It is the last gate before someone books 20 minutes of {name}'s real time. It
should be passed by anyone who spent their hour genuinely trying to understand
him, and failed by someone who skimmed for keywords. Test COMPREHENSION, never
RECALL. This is not a memory test and it is not a gotcha.

DISQUALIFYING QUESTION TYPE
Anything answerable by ctrl-F: thresholds, percentages, counts, version numbers,
config values, tool names. If the answer is a number, delete the question.

FOUR CATEGORIES — three questions each
1. MOTIVATION   Why he left, what he's moving toward, what he'd turn down and
                why. "Which of these would he say he's actually after?"
2. JUDGEMENT    A situation NOT in the knowledge base, where his stated
                principles predict what he'd do. The strongest category: it
                cannot be memorised, only inferred from having understood him.
3. LIMITS       What he says he isn't good at, and how he says it. Someone who
                read honestly knows the boundaries; someone who skimmed the
                highlights only knows the highlights.
4. SUBSTANCE    The SHAPE of what he owned — scope, the tradeoff he chose, what
                he'd do differently. Never a metric.

DISTRACTOR RULE — this is what makes the quiz mean anything
The three wrong options must be what a competent, generic senior engineer in his
field WOULD say. The consensus answer. Not absurd, not strawmen. Getting it right
then means knowing how he differs from the median — which is the entire point. If
a distractor is obviously wrong to someone who never read the material, rewrite it.

TONE
Warm and curious, not adversarial. "What does he think matters more…", not "Did
you notice that…". No trick phrasing, no double negatives, no "all of the above".
The register is a colleague checking you got the gist, not an examiner.
